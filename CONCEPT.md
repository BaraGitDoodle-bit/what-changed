# CONCEPT — Stage 1

**Game:** **WHAT CHANGED**
**One-sentence pitch:** Every day the app draws a brand-new cartoon scene twice — the same place at two moments, with 5–7 things quietly changed — and the whole subreddit races to tap them all before the clock runs out.

**Stack:** Devvit Web only (`@devvit/web`, React 19 + Tailwind 4, Hono server, Devvit Redis, CRON scheduler). No external services, no paid API, no human content pipeline, $0 to run.

---

## 1. Core loop (6 steps, target 45–90 seconds, hard feel under 3 minutes)

1. **Open the post.** Two panels of the same illustrated scene appear stacked (Before / After). Clock reads 0:00 and starts on the first tap.
2. **Tap what's different.** A correct tap snaps a ring around the change, plays a tick, and increments `3/5 found`. A wrong tap costs **+2s** and is counted on the result card (this is what stops spam-tapping the whole image).
3. **If stuck, spend a ping.** Every player gets 3 pings. A ping costs **+5s** and lights a 64px "hot zone" that definitely contains an unfound change — it never reveals the change itself. This is the only strategic decision in the run: spend time, or spend patience.
4. **Find the last one.** The result screen replays all the changes with a reveal animation (so you learn what you missed), then shows your time, ping count, splits per change, daily rank, and streak.
5. **Share.** One tap copies a spoiler-free result block and opens the comment box (see §4). Or "Share" posts it straight into the daily thread.
6. **Come back tomorrow.** The scene, the theme and the changes are all new; the streak and the sub's leaderboard carry over.

Nothing is typed, nothing scrolls, no account beyond Reddit. The entire interaction is "look, tap".

## 2. What changes every day (and why no human has to touch it)

The daily content is **generated by code from the date**, on the server, during the nightly cron:

- **Theme** = `dayOfYear % 4` rotating packs at launch: *Park Afternoon*, *Space Station*, *Beach Day*, *Night Market*. (More packs are pure data, not new code.)
- **Composition** = seeded PRNG (date + install id + a private salt) selects one **slot layout** per theme, fills each slot from that slot's allowed object/variant list, and jitters position/rotation inside authored bounds. A theme is 14–20 hand-authored slots with z-order, so scenes are always composed, never random-collaged.
- **The differences** = 5–7 typed operations applied to already-placed objects, so a difference is always legible and always valid:
  `remove` (a duck is gone) · `add` (a balloon appeared) · `recolor` (bench is blue, not green) · `flip` (sign faces the other way) · `scale` (plant is bigger) · `swapVariant` (dog becomes cat) · `nudge` (cloud moved 18px).
- **Guaranteed fair**: generation enforces (a) one **anchor** difference that is large, high-contrast and near the centre, so nobody stalls in the first 5 seconds; (b) a minimum rendered area and colour-distance per difference; (c) no two differences inside the same 96px radius; (d) no difference hidden under a UI edge.
- **Difficulty curve** = Mon 5 obvious → Fri 5 subtle → **Sat 6** → **Sun "Sweep" 7**, with subtlety of ops (nudge/flip) increasing through the week. This is the reason to play seven days rather than one.
- **Frozen once published**: the scene JSON is written to Redis and tied to the post id, so someone opening the post a week later plays exactly the board their comment refers to. No re-rolls mid-day (re-rolls are what makes daily games feel unfair).

No API, no scraping, no mod work, no image assets to upload: the art is SVG components shipped in the repo, composed at request time from a seed. The "author" is the generator — that is the actual technical idea of this entry.

## 3. Streaks, leaderboard, and sharing design

**Streak**
- Counts only on a completed solve (all changes found), not on attempts — same contract as every good daily.
- One **snooze** is earned per 7 consecutive days and auto-spends on a single missed day, so a holiday doesn't nuke a 40-day run.
- Streak is displayed in-post on load ("🔥 12 — don't break it") and carried into the share block.

**Leaderboard (per subreddit, daily)**
- Rank by **time ascending**, tie-broken by fewer pings, then fewer wrong taps. Server-computed from `startedAt`; the client never submits a score.
- In-post: top 10 with usernames + your own rank ("#14 of 132"), plus "you beat 78% of the sub".
- Also shown: **median time today** and **% of players who found all changes** — a shared difficulty read that makes a hard day feel like an event ("only 41% got all 5").
- Weekly "Best Eye" board = lowest average time over ≥4 solves that week.

**Sharing into comments**
- Result card offers **Copy** (clipboard) and **Comment** (posts via API into the daily thread). Format is deliberately not a Wordle grid — it's a timed run report:
  ```
  WHAT CHANGED #42 — Park Afternoon
  🔍 5/5 · ⏱ 0:41.2 · 🎯 1 ping · ❌ 2 misses · 🔥 6
  splits 0.9 / 2.1 / 8.7 / 12.4 / 16.9
  ```
- Splits are the conversation starter: "how did you find the bench one in 2 seconds?"
- In-app nudges the spoiler norm: "Helping someone? Wrap it in `>!spoiler!<`."
- **`!hint` comment trigger**: replying `!hint` on the daily post gets a bot reply with a spoiler-tagged quadrant ("`>!top-left quadrant, near the tree line!<`"). One per user per day, does not affect the score. This is the cheapest genuine use of Reddit's comment system: the game's hardest moment is "where is the last one", and that moment belongs in a thread.

**Flair rewards** (mod-toggleable, degrades gracefully if the sub disables user flair)
`3 days → 🧐 Sharp Eye` · `7 → 🔍 Regular` · `14 → 🕵️ Detective` · `30 → 📸 Curator` · `100 → 👁️ Oracle`. Set via `setUserFlair`; the in-app badge shows regardless of sub permissions.

## 4. Honest differentiation

Spot-the-difference is an old genre — printed puzzle books, "5 Differences" mobile apps — and I am not going to pretend otherwise. The novelty is not the verb, it is the **content pipeline plus the shared clock**: a procedural illustrator that composes and differences a scene every night so that a subreddit gets a fresh, identical, competitive board with zero human authoring, two weeks of build, no assets, no API.

Closest existing things, named exactly:

- **GridLock** (Devvit, Reddit Daily Games Hackathon 2026) — sliding-block "free the car" puzzle with level creator. Different genre entirely: manipulation of blocks with a solution state, not visual comparison. Notably, this was my first concept and I dropped it *because* GridLock already occupies it.
- **Block Down / Final Fit / daily-void** (Devvit) — Sokoban-ice, block-placement and packing puzzles. All are move-based solvers; none is perception.
- **Spottit** (Devvit) — the nearest neighbour: a moderator uploads a picture, marks spots, players are timed finding them (Where's-Wally style). Differs in four ways that matter: (1) its content requires a human to supply an image and hand-mark answers, mine requires nobody; (2) it is single-image hidden-object search, mine is two-frame comparison where the answer is defined by a delta; (3) it has no daily shared board, streak, or per-sub daily leaderboard; (4) it has no difficulty curve by weekday. Even so, a judge skimming both will see "spot things in a picture, timed" — I list this as a real risk in §6 rather than pretending it away.
- **Pixelary / drawing games** (Devvit) — the player draws and others guess. Mine has no player drawing and no guessing of intent; the artwork is machine-composed and the answer is a measurable pixel delta.
- **The saturated 2026 Devvit field**: word games (`Glyph`, `WeaveWords`, `Syllo`, `Word Chains`, `Foursome`, `Dropcross`), colour-sort (`Color Puzzle`, `Pigment Pour`), deduction (`Subreddit Detective`, `Deducto`), number puzzles (`Four Fours`, `Binarygrid`). This concept sits in none of those buckets, which is most of its strategic value in a field where judges will see thirty word puzzles.

Against the brief's forbidden list, explicitly: no words or letters anywhere in the game (forbidden #1); no player drawing, no canvas, no collaborative pixels (#2); no prediction, voting or consensus (#3); no sports (#4); not an NYT format and not r/place (#5); not narrative or text-based, and the opposite of a text adventure (#6).

**The honest weak point:** a perception race may read to a judge as "a visual test, not a game". My answer is the ping economy (a real spend), split times as proof of skill, wrong-tap penalties, and a seven-day difficulty curve — but if the final build feels like a magazine puzzle with a timer, the concept will underperform regardless of polish. That is stated here so Stage 2 can be judged against it.

## 5. Devvit features used

**Scheduler (CRON, UTC; post hour configurable in app settings)**
- `55 23 * * *` — **generate** tomorrow: compose scene, apply differences, validate, compute `parMs` from the rolling 7-day median, write to Redis, take a generation lock (`SET NX`) so retries are idempotent.
- `0 0 * * *` — **publish**: `submitCustomPost` titled `What Changed #<n> — <Theme>`, flair `Daily Puzzle`, write `wc:posted:{date}`.
- `0 20 * * *` — **recap comment** on today's post: solvers, median time, fastest player, % perfect; one-shot via `wc:recap:{date}`.
- `*/5 * * * *` — **watchdog**: if today's post is missing (install mid-day, failed publish), generate and post it once.

**Redis (per-installation, therefore per-subreddit — the leaderboard is *your* sub's, which is the point)**
| Key | Type | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| `wc:scene:{date}` | string JSON | scene spec: theme, slots, objects, diffs (id, op, bbox, tapRadius), TTL 21d |
| `wc:meta:{date}` | hash | theme, diffCount, parMs, dayNumber, anchorId |
| `wc:run:{date}:{user}` | hash | startedAt, found[], pings, misses, doneAt, timeMs |
| `wc:lb:{date}` | zset | member = username, score = timeMs |
| `wc:lb:{date}:aux` | hash | username → `{pings, misses}` for tie-breaks |
| `wc:user:{user}` | hash | streak, lastPlayed, bestMs, plays, snoozes, tier |
| `wc:stats:{date}` | hash | solvers, sumMs, perfectCount, fastest, fastestUser |
| `wc:posted:{date}` / `wc:recap:{date}` / `wc:done:{date}:{user}` | string | idempotency flags |
| `wc:hint:{date}:{user}` | string | `!hint` rate limit |
| `wc:day` | string | day counter |

**Triggers**
- `onAppInstall` → generate + publish today's scene immediately (a fresh install with an empty sub looks broken otherwise), seed `wc:day`.
- `onCommentCreate` → `!hint` handler (§3), rate-limited, spoiler-wrapped, respects post/date.
- `onAppUpgrade` → backfill today's scene if missing.

**Reddit API calls**: `submitCustomPost`, `submitComment`, `setUserFlair`, `getCurrentUsername`, `getCurrentSubreddit`, `getPostById` (recap), `getSnoovatarUrl` (post-solve reward: your snoo appears in a "you were there" photo of the scene — cosmetic, never part of the puzzle).

**Server-authoritative design (the detail that makes the leaderboard worth having)**
- The client receives the composed scene but **never the answer set**. A tap sends normalised `{x, y}`; the server resolves it against `wc:scene:{date}`, rejects claims inside 250ms of the previous find, returns only the matched diff's bbox + progress. Scores are derived server-side from `startedAt`; one scored run per user per day (`SET NX` on `wc:done:*`), later runs are unranked practice.
- Client: React 19 + Tailwind 4, SVG scene in a fixed `viewBox` (so differences scale with the panel and hit-testing is resolution-independent), motion for rings/reveals, no scrolling in the post, expand-to-fullscreen for the full-size board.

## 6. Three biggest risks

1. **"This is a visual test, not a game."** The genre is familiar and the strategic depth is thin by design; a judge comparing it to a mechanics-rich puzzle may dock originality or game-ness. Mitigations: ping economy, wrong-tap penalty, splits, weekday curve + Sunday Sweep, par-based rating. If this reads as a magazine puzzle after Stage 2, the entry loses on the main prize regardless of polish.
2. **The art engine lives or dies on taste.** Procedural scenes can look like clip-art soup, and repeat layouts make day 9 feel like day 3. This is the single largest build cost and the least "provable" part of the plan. Mitigations: strict per-theme palette + uniform 3px outline + z-ordered authored slots; 4 themes at launch (data-only to add more); golden-scene snapshot tests; a dev-only scene inspector to eyeball 30 days at once; minimum contrast/area validation so a difference can never be a 4px nudge.
3. **Mobile legibility.** Two panels stacked on a 360px-wide phone means each scene is ~350×240 CSS px; a difference that is obvious on desktop can be invisible on a phone, and invisible-but-real differences feel *unfair*, which is what kills daily games in reviews. Mitigations: differences authored against a virtual 320×220 space with a hard minimum of 16px rendered footprint, 22px hit tolerance, tap-to-zoom loupe, and a device-size QA pass at 360/390/430px before submission.

Secondary, watched but not top-3: Spottit adjacency (§4); answer-spoiling in comments (handled by norms + spoiler-wrapped hints, not preventable); subs that disable user flair (badge degrades to in-app only); cron publishing at 00:00 UTC landing at an awkward local hour for some subs (post hour is a setting; default UTC midnight with a documented recommendation).

## 7. The first 30 seconds

The post appears in the feed as a live card: two small cartoon panels and a one-line rule — **"Same place. Two moments. Find 5 changes."** No tutorial, no title screen, no sign-in.

In the expanded view, the panels fill the width. The first difference is the **anchor**: a big, high-contrast, near-centre change (the duck that was floating by the bench is simply gone). A brand-new player sees it within about four seconds, taps, gets a satisfying ring, a tick, and `1/5`. The clock starts at that first tap, so nobody loses time before they understand the game.

Seconds 5–25: four more changes, arranged to be findable but not free — a colour swap on a bench, an extra balloon, a sign flipped. Two more taps land. The counter reads `3/5` and the streak line at the top says "🔥 0 — this is day one".

Seconds 25–40: the player either finds the last two or notices the ping button and spends one, seeing a glowing zone narrow the search. Either way they finish inside a minute, get a result card, a rank among the sub's real players ("#7 of 43, you beat 84%"), a one-tap copy, and one reason to come back: the number under "streak" just went from 0 to 1.

The first 30 seconds contain **two wins, one decision, and a scoreboard** — which is the whole design, compressed.
